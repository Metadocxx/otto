Story of Lord Krishna

Lord Krishna, one of the most beloved deities in Hinduism, was born in Mathura to Devaki and Vasudeva. His birth was no ordinary event — it was prophesied that Devaki’s eighth son would destroy the tyrant king Kansa. To protect the divine child, Vasudeva carried the newborn Krishna across the Yamuna River to Gokul, where he was raised by Nanda and Yashoda.

Krishna’s childhood was filled with enchanting miracles. He is remembered as the mischievous “Makhan Chor” (butter thief), stealing butter from the homes of Gokul and delighting the villagers with his playful nature. Yet, behind this mischief lay profound lessons — teaching that the divine can be found in joy, innocence, and love.

One of the most famous episodes from his youth is the lifting of Govardhan Hill. When the people of Vrindavan faced torrential rains sent by Indra, Krishna raised the entire hill with his little finger, sheltering the villagers and cattle beneath it. This act symbolized his role as protector and reminded humanity that devotion and humility are greater than pride and arrogance.

As he grew, Krishna became the central figure in the Mahabharata. Serving as Arjuna’s charioteer, he delivered the timeless wisdom of the Bhagavad Gita. In it, Krishna taught the importance of duty (dharma), selfless action, and devotion to God. His words continue to guide millions, offering clarity in times of confusion and strength in moments of despair.

Krishna’s life is not just a tale of miracles and battles; it is a story of divine love. His bond with Radha represents the eternal union of the soul with the divine, reminding devotees that true devotion transcends worldly boundaries.

Lesson: Krishna’s story teaches us that life should be lived with joy, courage, and devotion. Through his playful childhood, protective acts, and profound wisdom, he shows that the divine is both a loving companion and a guiding teacher.
